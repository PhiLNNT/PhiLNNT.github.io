## Abstract

We present monotone finite-difference schemes for second-order nonlinear elliptic equations on a finite junction, with Dirichlet conditions at the exterior vertices and a Kirchhoff condition at the junction; see Barles, Ley and Topp, "Degenerate Elliptic PDEs on a Network with Kirchhoff Conditions" (2025), for a more general analytical framework. In contrast with fully coupled discretizations on the whole junction, such as Morfe, "Convergence & rates for Hamilton-Jacobi equations with Kirchhoff junction conditions" (2020), we propose a decoupling strategy: for a prescribed junction value, independent Dirichlet problems are solved on the branches by monotone finite-difference schemes inspired by Crandall and Lions, "Two approximations of solutions of Hamilton-Jacobi equations" (1984). The junction value is then recovered from a scalar nonlinear flux-balance equation. This construction preserves the one-dimensional structure of the branch problems and is well suited to extensions to larger networks. We establish first-order convergence for both the branch solutions and the discrete derivatives. The resulting reconstruction yields a first-order convergence rate for the full junction problem. We illustrate the method for Hamiltonians of absolute-value type using Lax–Friedrichs and upwind numerical approximations. The nonlinear algebraic systems are treated using semismooth Newton and Howard-type methods, together with techniques related to Daniilidis, Haddou, Le, Ley and Tran, "Solving Nonlinear Absolute Value Equations" (2026).
